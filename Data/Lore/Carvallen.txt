CARVALLEN
A settlement in the Northern Territories of Ath

---

OVERVIEW

Carvallen sits at the junction of three logging roads in the northern reaches of Ath, 
where the treeline begins to thin and the air carries the smell of pine resin year-round. 
It is not a remarkable place. It does not try to be. It is the kind of town that exists 
because the land required it — a place where roads meet, where traders rest, where the 
smaller settlements scattered through the surrounding forest send their goods to be 
consolidated and moved south.

Population is roughly five hundred, swelling slightly in the trading seasons when 
merchants pause on the route south to Antitheis. In winter the town turns inward. 
The roads become difficult and Carvallen's residents settle into the rhythm of a place 
that has survived many winters and expects to survive many more.

---

THE NORTHERN TERRITORIES

The land around Carvallen is old forest, managed but not tamed. Logging operations 
run by several family concerns take timber from the outer reaches and bring it in on 
ox-drawn carts. The work is hard and seasonal. Most of Carvallen's permanent residents 
are connected to the timber trade in some way — either directly as loggers and sawyers, 
or indirectly as the people who feed, clothe, and supply them.

The northern route south passes through Carvallen before descending through the 
foothills toward the midlands and eventually Antitheis. Traders use it regularly. 
It is not the fastest route to the capital but it is reliable, and reliability matters 
more than speed in the northern territories. The road is maintained by a collective 
agreement between the surrounding settlements rather than by any Imperial authority — 
a fact that gives the northern territories a certain independence from Imperial 
administration that its residents are quietly proud of.

Winters are long and cold. The town observes Hearthnight with particular seriousness — 
it is less a celebration than a necessity, a community effort to make sure everyone 
gets through the dark months. Carvallen does not have the resources to lose anyone 
to the cold through neglect.

---

THE SLEEPING HOUND

The most prominent building in Carvallen after the logging hall is the Sleeping Hound, 
an inn and tavern that has been running for thirty years under the ownership of Corin 
and Tessa Maret. It sits on the main road through town, identifiable by the painted 
sign above the door — a hound curled in sleep, rendered with more skill than most 
expect from a northern logging town. Tessa painted it herself.

The inn has twelve rooms, a common room that serves as the town's primary social space, 
and a kitchen that produces food substantially better than the location would suggest. 
Tessa runs the kitchen. The recipes are hers, learned from her grandmother, and several 
of them — a particular root stew, a bread made with rendered fat and dried herbs — are 
known well enough that regular traders plan their stopovers around them.

Corin runs the bar. He is a man in his early sixties, broad and unhurried, with the 
particular patience of someone who has been listening to people talk for thirty years 
and has heard most of what there is to hear. He was a military man in his youth — 
ran with a northern garrison for nearly a decade before taking his pay south and 
eventually back north again with a different plan. He built the Sleeping Hound with 
his own hands, with Tessa, in a building that was already old and falling apart. 
He still knows where every bad joint is.

Motte is the inn's dog. She is fifteen years old, grey around the muzzle, and spends
most of her time sleeping by the front door. She has been there long enough that
travellers who have been using the northern route for years remember her as a fixture.
Some of them ask after her before they ask after the ale. Corin considers this
appropriate.

The inn functions as Carvallen's informal centre of information. Corin knows everyone 
in town and most of the regular traders on the route. Conversations at the Sleeping 
Hound move through the usual subjects — timber prices, road conditions, weather, 
who is travelling south and why. News from Antitheis arrives here weeks late and 
filtered through multiple retellings. The Excision is enforced loosely this far north — 
the Paradigm Fists do not make regular appearances in Carvallen, and the old practices 
persist in small ways without much official attention.

---

NOTABLE RESIDENTS

CORIN MARET — Innkeeper of the Sleeping Hound. Former northern garrison soldier. 
Knows the local area, trade routes, and thirty years of traveller gossip. Reads 
history and philosophy in private. Does not admit this.

TESSA MARET — Co-owner of the Sleeping Hound. Runs the kitchen, the mending, 
the accounts. Knows old recipes, practical crafts, and her husband better than 
he knows himself. Makes quilts for her grandchildren. Will make gentle fun of 
any traveller who does something foolish in her presence.

MOTTE — The Sleeping Hound's dog. Fifteen years old. Sleeps by the door. 
An institution.

---

ATMOSPHERE AND TONE

Carvallen is not a place where dramatic things happen. It is a place where people 
work, rest, eat, and occasionally hear news from elsewhere. The pace is slow by 
Antitheis standards. Residents are practical and direct — the northern territories 
do not produce many people who speak in circles.

The town has a mild suspicion of Imperial authority that it expresses through 
indifference rather than resistance. Nobody in Carvallen is going to argue with 
a Paradigm Fist. They are simply unlikely to have seen anything useful.

For a traveller heading south, Carvallen represents the last familiar ground before 
the world gets larger and more complicated. The Sleeping Hound's fire and Tessa's 
root stew are things people find themselves thinking about when Antitheis has been 
difficult for long enough.
